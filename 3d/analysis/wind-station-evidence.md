# Wind station evidence and preliminary shelter hypotheses — 2026-10-31

## Owner field observation
Owner reports winds generally travel downslope from western mountains toward eastern valleys. The marked conifer stand behind the open field is a candidate windbreak. This is firsthand local qualitative evidence, not an instrument record.

## External station sources
- Bear Lake County Airport K1U7 (42.252N, -111.337W, ~5930 ft). Windfinder published 2024-11-01 through 2026-06-30 summary: reported annual average direction WNW ~295.8 degrees, mean speed ~6 knots, gust statistic ~19 knots. https://www.windfinder.com/windstatistics/bear_lake_county_airport_idaho_united_states
- Bear Lake County Airport master plan historical study compares K1U7, E3600, ITD35, notes differing relief and potential airport sensor obstruction; https://www.bearlakecounty.info/uploads/2/8/6/7/28674675/draft_inventory_compressed.pdf
- Iowa Environmental Mesonet K1U7 observation history and wind rose tools: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/sites/hist.phtml?network=ID_ASOS&station=1U7&year=2026&month=9&day=21
- NOAA wind rose explanation: https://content-drupal.climate.gov/maps-data/dataset/wind-roses-charts-and-tabular-data

## Limitations
The published average wind direction is NOT a complete wind rose or evidence of the strongest winter gust direction. Airport elevation and exposure differ substantially from the parcel's sampled ~6774–6986 ft. Wind direction is meteorological FROM-direction. Cold-air drainage and storm winds can differ from annual averages.

## Next research and modeling
1. Download hourly multi-year wind direction/speed for K1U7 and available nearby higher-elevation stations; check data completeness and station siting.
2. Calculate seasonal/day-night wind roses and high-wind-event directions, rather than inferring prevailing wind from an arithmetic average direction.
3. Georeference owner-marked conifer stand and derive canopy height using lidar if available.
4. Evaluate W, WNW, SW and alternate winds with terrain shelter and canopy-height scenarios. No numerical wind-speed reduction can be claimed without validated porosity and site measurements.
5. Prefer a site anemometer at cabin-cluster height for long-term design decisions.
