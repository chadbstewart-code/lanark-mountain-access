# Member-funded cabins and shared infrastructure — Lanark concept

Recorded: 2026-10-31. Status: Owner-stated design goals, **not approved development**.

## Proposed economics
- One undivided 55.75-acre parcel (PIN RP13S43E185400), reportedly Agricultural zoning.
- Four to eight small cabins, each assigned to a particular member or members.
- Each member pays for construction and maintenance of their assigned cabin(s).
- LLC or TIC participants share common expenses, especially roads and a water well.
- Members hold differing or equal percentage interests and intend to transfer/sell interests with assigned cabin rights at market value.
- Primary use is private recreation; a genuine business operation may be considered only if it lawfully improves the approval path.
- Owner reports subdivision is unavailable except possible direct-child exception; requires independent verification.

## Unresolved legal and practical questions
1. If LLC owns fee title, attached cabins may be improvements to LLC real property notwithstanding individual funding. Determine legally enforceable improvements rights, capital credits, buyout and insurance.
2. Exclusive assigned cabin sites coupled with independent resale may implicate county subdivision rules or other real estate regulation. Obtain written county interpretation before marketing interests.
3. Confirm zoning, allowable cabin count and occupancy, permit classification and whether commercial guest/dude ranch is genuinely applicable.
4. Shared well: confirm Idaho water rights, permitted beneficial uses, well authorization, drinking-water system classification and required testing/treatment; multiple independent cabins may change regulatory treatment.
5. Shared roads: legal rights-of-way, fire apparatus access, grading, drainage, snow removal, maintenance and capital replacement reserve.
6. Allocate infrastructure contributions fairly where member cabins differ in size, usage or location.
7. Address default, liens, construction deadlines, unfinished cabins, sale approvals, valuation, right of first refusal, inheritance, disputes, lender consent and dissolution.
8. Engage Idaho land-use counsel and tax advisor before any deed transfer, membership offering or cabin construction.

## Planning recommendation (not legal advice)
Evaluate one owner LLC with documented membership interests and separately recorded/contractual cabin-use allocations against TIC and other lawful models. County classification and land-use approval are gating issues; an LLC does not create an exemption.
